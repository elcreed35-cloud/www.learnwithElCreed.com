What is Surface Area?

Surface area is the total area of all the exposed surfaces of a 3D object.

Surface Area = Sum of all exposed surfaces
⭐ Exam trick: Look at the object and ask: “Which surfaces are exposed?” Add only those surfaces.

What is Volume?

Volume is the amount of space occupied by a 3D object.

Volume of a prism = Area of cross-section × Length

Cross-Sectional Area

A cross-section is the 2D shape revealed when a 3D object is cut.

Cross-sectionArea
RectangleA = lw
TriangleA = ½bh
CircleA = πr²
TrapeziumA = ½(a+b)h
For a uniform prism: A = Volume ÷ Length

🔓 Open vs Closed

Closed: include every surface.

Open on one side: leave out the missing surface.

Open on two sides: leave out both missing surfaces.

🧊 Explore Common 3D Shapes

Cube

SA = 6a²

V = a³

Cuboid

SA = 2(lw+lh+wh)

V = lwh

Cylinder

SA = 2πr(r+h)

V = πr²h

Sphere

SA = 4πr²

V = ⁴⁄₃πr³

Cone

SA = πr(r+l)

V = ⅓πr²h

Open Shapes

Cube, one face open: SA = 5a²
Cuboid, top open: SA = lw + 2lh + 2wh
Cylinder, one end open: SA = πr² + 2πrh
Cylinder, both ends open: SA = 2πrh
Prism, one end open: SA = A + PL
Prism, both ends open: SA = PL

✏️ Guided Practice

Example 1 — Cube

A cube has side 5 cm.

SA = 6a² = 6 × 5² = 150 cm²

V = a³ = 5³ = 125 cm³

Example 2 — Open-Top Cuboid

l = 5 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 2 cm.

Open-top SA = lw + 2lh + 2wh

= 15 + 20 + 12 = 47 cm²

Example 3 — Prism

Cross-sectional area = 8 cm² and length = 5 cm.

V = A × L = 8 × 5 = 40 cm³
